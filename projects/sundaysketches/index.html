Sunday Sketches

Illustration / Personal2025

A lifelong love of drawing, shared through a weekly tradition: Sunday Sketches.

The story

A tradition of art

Growing up, we always had a newspaper nearby, full of the latest headlines from local events to global news. On Sundays I’d race to the breakfast table to flip through the newspaper and grab the comics section. I’d read it over cereal, soaking in the humor and paying special attention to the unique style of the different illustrations. Then it was off to church.

Some of my earliest memories are of sketching during church, sitting beside my dad and sisters. Sometimes we went for serious drawings—portraits of the speaker or careful studies of the chapel’s architecture. More often, our sketches were playful. We were always trying to make each other, and anyone nearby, laugh during the service.

I can still picture my dad, a talented artist, sketching with effortless confidence as friends leaned over the pews to see what he was working on. Before long, I was the one drawing a crowd. Having a pencil in my hand helped me listen, stay focused, and, over time, taught me how to draw with an audience.

As I got older and became a dad myself, the tradition continued. I found myself sketching alongside my own children—and sure enough, their talents began to shine too. Now our house is full of sketchbooks and scraps of paper, all covered in doodles and drawings.

More recently, I began sharing a weekly drawing on social media—something I call Sunday Sketches. These have become a visual journal: playful, personal snapshots of family life, marriage, parenting, and everything in between.

These days, it’s my kids’ friends who lean over the pews to see what I’ve drawn. Every Sunday afternoon, my phone buzzes with messages from friends and family: “Can’t wait to see this week’s Sunday Sketch!”
