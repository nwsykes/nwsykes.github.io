Product / Workflow2020

The path that led to one of our first commercially successful machine-learning projects.

01

A popularity-based engine

I’m proud to have led, designed, and built one of the first commercially successful machine-learning projects in our company. Beginning in 2017, we used estimate data from across North America to predict line items and built a popularity-based engine.

We accurately determined common items for states and provinces based on loss type and rooms being worked on. Our first market iteration offered those items as suggestions before a user even searched a price list.

02

A leap forward

The feature helped a younger, less experienced workforce build estimates with minimal training. Estimates created with the feature also required fewer corrections and less expensive review from construction experts.

We added filters by work type, hints that improved accuracy, and continued refining the engine’s recommendations.

03

Recommendations in context

Eventually the engine could evaluate items already in a room and make more meaningful recommendations based on location, room type, loss type, and the work already entered.

Our final iteration introduced a subtle bottom sheet whenever an item was added. Enter carpet and the system recommends carpet pad; enter carpet pad and it suggests baseboard—the process continues until the estimate is complete.
