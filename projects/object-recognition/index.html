Object Recognition

Applied AI / Mobile2021

The story behind one of our company’s most exciting features—an experiment that began as a hack.

The story, the process

2018

From hack to product

In 2018 we started our first object-recognition feature during a hack week. It began as a simple prototype in the ContentsTrack mobile app connected to a commercial engine. Customer feedback quickly convinced us the feature had a bright future.

We created an industry-specific classification model—a convolutional neural network—using imagery from ContentsTrack. That specialized training produced impressive accuracy compared with general models trained on brand-new consumer items.

2019

Learning at scale

Next we brought the feature to a larger audience through Xactimate Mobile. We improved the feedback loop so the engine could learn from successful and unsuccessful recognition attempts, then connected recognized objects to regional price-list items, brands, and models.

We made the experience more accessible and invested heavily in UX, working closely with key customers and partners while the engine continued to become more robust.

2020

Multiple-object recognition

By early 2020 we were building an engine that could recognize multiple objects in a room very quickly. We explored intuitive ways to capture a single item or an entire room.

The system needed to work on-device in a fraction of a second as the user moved a camera through a space, while the interface captured items, quantities, and associated photos with minimal effort.

2021

Release and impact

In early 2021 we released our strongest iteration. Hundreds of thousands of items were captured and cataloged, saving countless hours for end users and homeowners.

I am a named inventor on the associated patent, Computer Vision Systems and Methods for Automatically Detecting, Classifying, and Pricing Objects Captured in Images or Videos.
